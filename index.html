Before all of this…

You were just someone I had seen around.

Pretty, obviously. And in my head, pretty always came with a certain amount of attitude. So I quietly decided you were out of bothering-range, said nothing, and went about my day.

And then came the workshop.

The workshop

A room full of tasks, and somewhere in it, you.

I ended up picking you for a task, and my first read was simple: she seems nice. Then came our first real conversation, and talking to you was easy. No performance, no guessing games. Genuinely sweet, genuinely respectful.

I didn't have a crush. Not yet. Just a thought that wouldn't leave: I actually like talking to this person.

The first little moments

Nothing that felt like the beginning of anything.

Your first day. You showed up late, half-expecting trouble, and I told you to just mention my name to Vivek sir. It smoothed things over. Looking back, it was the first time we showed up for each other, even in the smallest way.

29 August – September

The hard days

Not everything about this month was easy. On 29 August your badge was taken because of constant gossip. On 3 September mine was taken too, I was scolded a lot, stopped by the principal, and got home late. Someone even called your home. Then the school got loud, everyone complaining, everyone watching, and the half-yearly didn't go well either.

I kept asking why it was always the two of us.

But through all of it, there was one thing that stayed good: you. You gave me a reason to smile again, and you are the reason I keep moving forward every single day.

Hard days passed. You stayed.

14 September

The day that mattered most

Days before, someone had told your family we were always together and that you should not be sent on the 14th, the day of the Ganesh puja. There were threats too. It felt like the whole plan was over before it began.

You still came. All day, people kept an eye on me, and there was hardly a chance to talk. I kept wondering why I had even come to school. Only during Pushpanjali did we get a little time, a few words.

Then, on the bus, we sat together, clicked a few pictures, and stayed side by side for a very long time. My hand on your shoulder, your hand in mine. Nothing was said out loud, and it was the calmest part of that whole month.

It didn't end quietly. Your mother saw, she was angry, and you had to face the consequences at home. I couldn't do anything about it, and I hated that.

But I would still choose that bus ride, with you.
